# Getting started

Decay archives the browser tabs and hides the app windows you've stopped using, and brings any of them back with one shortcut. Setting it up takes about five minutes.

## 1. Install the app

[Download Decay](/download) for macOS (14 or later) or Windows (10 or 11) and open it. A short guide walks you through the rest.

## 2. Choose a pace

How long a tab may go unused before Decay acts, in **active hours** (time only counts while you're using your computer):

| Pace | Stale after | Window hidden after | Archived after |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gentle | 24 h | 60 h | 96 h |
| Balanced (default) | 12 h | 36 h | 60 h |
| Aggressive | 8 h | 24 h | 40 h |

You can change it any time in Settings → Aging, or set your own hours.

## 3. Add the browser extension

The guide lists the browsers it found and opens the extension's page in each. When the extension is installed, click **Connect to the Decay app** in its popup. See [Browsers and the extension](/docs/browsers).

## 4. Allow window access (optional)

To hide app windows you've stopped using, Decay needs **Accessibility** access on macOS (System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility). Without it, Decay works with tabs only. Windows doesn't ask.

## 5. A week of shadow mode

For the first seven days Decay closes nothing: it shows you, in the Library's **Shadow review**, what it *would* have archived. When you're happy with its judgement, switch to **Automatic** in the menu bar (Windows: the tray icon) or in Settings → General.

## The shortcut to remember

**⌥⌘D** (Windows: **Ctrl+Alt+D**) opens the search panel from anywhere. Type a few letters, press **Return**, and the tab is back. More in [Search and restore](/docs/search-and-restore).
